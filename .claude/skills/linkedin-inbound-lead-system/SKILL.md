# LinkedIn organic inbound lead system

Core reframe: LinkedIn is not a resume or a place for cold DMs — it's a
content distribution engine + trust-building machine + sales funnel
disguised as a professional network. Six layers, in order, each layer
useless without the one before it converting.

## Layer 1 — Profile architecture (treat it as a landing page)

If the profile doesn't convert a visitor in the first few seconds, all
content driving traffic to it is wasted:

- **Photo**: headshot only, face ~60% of frame. No group photos, full-body
  shots, sunglasses, heavy filters.
- **Banner**: clean value prop in the right half only (never text under/
  behind the profile picture). Who you help + what you do, not corporate
  stock imagery.
- **Headline formula**: `[what you do/delivery mechanism] + [social proof
  number] + [who you help/ICP]`. e.g. "Scaled 47 e-commerce brands past $5M
  with email automation." Every headline should answer: what do you do, who
  do you help, how, and why should they believe you.
- **Custom button** (via LinkedIn Sales Navigator settings): link straight
  to a booking page (Cal.com/Calendly) or a newsletter signup — remove
  friction between profile visit and next action.
- **About section**: speak to the ICP's pain, not your résumé/credentials.
  Same what/who/how/proof structure as the headline, expanded.
- **Featured section**: curated, not a link dump. Pattern that works: (1)
  direct booking/consultation link, (2) your best-performing viral posts,
  (3) a free resource/template/PDF (gated for engagement), (4) a personal
  story/background post — people buy from people once they know you.

## Layer 2 — Content mix (traffic mechanism)

Post types map to funnel stage; don't post only one type:

- **Giveaway / lead magnet posts** — highest-value type; gate a resource
  behind a comment trigger word (see Layer 5).
- **Thought leadership posts** — frameworks/ideas from your own experience,
  not generic tips.
- **Technical breakdowns** — walk through your product/service/system in
  detail.

Funnel logic: reach (giveaway/thought-leadership posts) → relevance
(comments trigger auto-DM nurture) → convert (CTA to book a call).

## Layer 3 — Hook mastery

Only the first 1-2 lines + the visual asset get seen before "see more."
If they don't stop the scroll in ~2 seconds, the rest of the post never
gets read. Formulas that work, with the mechanism they exploit:

- **Numbers/crunch hook** — specific figures create credibility + an
  information gap. "These 6 AI agents outperformed my $280K/year marketing
  team." "43,000 LinkedIn followers → $370K in 6 months."
- **Bold claim hook** — pattern interrupt + controversy. "Never use
  ChatGPT." "Stop hiring content creators."
- **Breaking news hook** — FOMO + urgency, ride a just-released tool/model.
  "Breaking: [tool] just dropped [feature]." Being first mover matters more
  than post quality here.
- **Trust/authority builder hook** — borrow or stack credibility + implied
  effort. "After 2,000 hours of testing, I built..." "I compiled [known
  expert]'s 422 videos into a custom GPT."
- **Outcome + number hook** — combine trending tool names with a dollar/time
  outcome. "[Tool A] + [Tool B] + [Tool C] = $X in 5 days."

**Post anatomy**: hook (1-2 lines) → visual asset → body structured as
problem → insight → solution → proof (specific numbers/tools, no fluff) →
CTA ("comment X to unlock", "DM me for access", scarcity framing) →
formatting (short lines, whitespace, bold key points, mobile-skim-friendly)
→ timing (post 8-9am EST — when US decision-makers are online).

## Layer 4 — Visual asset strategy by funnel stage

Images/carousels/video increase dwell time, which the algorithm rewards
with wider distribution. Match visual type to funnel stage:

- **Top of funnel (reach)**: giveaway/educational posts with a dynamic
  screen recording or gated Notion doc preview — perceived value + FOMO.
- **Middle of funnel (relevance)**: face-to-camera video (1-5 min, 15 min
  max on LinkedIn) building trust by showing you/the system in action.
- **Bottom of funnel (convert)**: case studies, before/after result
  dashboards, high-contrast static screenshots — proof that converts.

Audit every post on mobile after publishing — that's where most viewers
read it.

## Layer 5 — Auto-DM nurture system (don't let comments die)

A viral post can generate thousands of comments no one can DM manually —
untouched comments are lost leads. Set up comment-trigger automation
(e.g. a tool like Lead Shark):

1. Post asks people to comment a specific trigger word.
2. Automation detects the trigger word and DMs connected commenters the
   resource automatically, and prompts non-connections to connect first.
3. The auto-DM includes a qualifying question (separates tire-kickers from
   buyers) and opens a non-salesy conversation.
4. Nurture sequence → book-call CTA. This runs 24/7 without manual work.

## Layer 6 — Algorithm mechanics

- **Rule of one (niche)**: stay in one lane; the algorithm categorizes you
  by niche and rewards consistency within it.
- **Network-based distribution**: LinkedIn shows the post to 1st-degree
  connections first; their engagement (comments > likes, dwell time,
  reposts, bookmarks) determines push to 2nd/3rd-degree — that's what
  "going viral" actually is.
- **Consistency beats perfect timing**: 8-9am EST is a good default, but
  posting daily matters more than hitting the exact minute.
- **First-hour engagement velocity**: reply to every comment in the first
  60 minutes — the algorithm weighs this heavily.
- **Comments > likes** for distribution — engineer for comments via
  giveaways/questions, not just likes.
- **Ride trends**: be first to post about a new model/tool/industry news.
  Keep an AI "ghostwriter" system pre-trained on your voice and viral
  formats so you can publish within the trend's first hours, not days
  later.

## Applying it

Work the layers in order for a new profile: architecture (1) before
content (2) before hooks/visuals (3-4) before automation (5) before
optimizing for algorithm reach (6) — driving traffic to an unoptimized
profile or un-instrumented comments wastes the reach.
